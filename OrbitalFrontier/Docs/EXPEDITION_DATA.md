# Expedition terrain, science and flight model

## Playable regions and data chain

| World | Original elevation and imagery | Playable representation |
|---|---|---|
| Moon — Hadley–Apennine | NASA LRO/LROC `NAC_DTM_APOLLO15_2`, 5 m stereo elevation. LROC orthophoto `M1121224102_5M`. The source’s adjusted digital terrain model was published 27 August 2019, using images acquired in April 2013. [Product record](https://data.lroc.im-ldi.com/lroc/view_rdr/NAC_DTM_APOLLO15_2) · [NASA landing coordinates](https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/static/history/SP-4029/Apollo_18-27_LM_Lunar_Landing.htm) | Crop is 361 × 361 cell centers (1.8 × 1.8 km), centered at 26.132254° N, 3.633826° E. Source pixels start at column 1678, row 4299. Center elevation is −1,928.661 m relative to the LRO reference sphere. Unity samples the visible mesh at 10 m and the browser at about 10.2 m. The simulation interpolates the bundled 5 m elevations. |
| Mars — Jezero Crater | NASA/JPL and USGS Mars 2020 Science Investigation CTX DEM mosaic (20 m) and matched 5 m orbital orthoimage. Stereo DEMs were tied to MGS MOLA vertically. Publication date 14 October 2021. [USGS DEM record](https://astrogeology.usgs.gov/search/map/mars_2020_science_investigation_ctx_dem_mosaic) · [Research and landing position](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20230016428/downloads/Bell%20et%20al%202022sciadv.pdf) | Crop is 151 × 151 cell centers (3.0 × 3.0 km), centered at 18.444801° N, 77.450813° E, near the Perseverance landing area. Source pixels start at column 1199, row 2430. Center elevation is −2,567.054 m in the published mosaic’s vertical datum. |

Coordinates use the source equirectangular grids. Rows run south and columns run east. The source crops were checked for NoData values, then stored as little-endian signed 16-bit deltas from the center elevation in quarter-metres. Geometry and collision sample the same elevations. Walking height, landing contact and field slope use bilinear height interpolation and centered finite differences. Relief uses a vertical scale of one within the tile. Orthophotos were resampled to 768 × 768 and their grayscale was contrast-adjusted and tinted for the scene. Color tones, beacons, structures and the distant surround are illustrative. Hazards below the source cell size are outside the terrain model.

The Moon globes use the [NASA CGI Moon Kit](https://svs.gsfc.nasa.gov/4720/). Mars uses [Arizona State University’s Viking merged color mosaic](https://www.mars.asu.edu/data/mdim_color/), reassembled from its browse tiles. Earth uses the [March 2004 Blue Marble Next Generation global map](https://science.nasa.gov/earth/earth-observatory/blue-marble-next-generation/base-map/). Physical constants use [JPL DE440](https://ssd.jpl.nasa.gov/astro_par.html) and NASA planetary fact sheets. Apollo photos are credited in [the original data register](DATA_AND_MODEL.md). The game also references the [Apollo 15 geologic sample study](https://pubs.usgs.gov/publication/70198181), [Apollo 15 surface traverse](https://science.nasa.gov/photojournal/retracing-the-steps-of-apollo-15/), and [NASA’s Perseverance mission research](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/mars-2020-perseverance/). The waypoints are game stations. The Jezero delta is outside the tile. Field elevation and slope come from the source raster. Packet sizes, instrument bonuses and any inferred observations are game mechanics.

## What is simulated

- **Mission design** — Wet mass, center of mass, budget, launcher delivery class, reserve Δv, thrust-to-weight, generation, active load, battery, storage, downlink and crew endurance change when hardware or route changes. The three launchers and spacecraft are fictional design classes with explicit in-game payload limits. The three mission objectives have different science, descent and repair requirements.
- **Flight** — Ascent is an authored two-stage profile with player-controlled staging. Cruise uses configurable compressed flight times and a scenario-specific capture budget. Capture expends propellant with the rocket equation. Local orbit uses numerical two-body propagation with Mars/Moon GM, eclipse geometry and tangential course trims. Transfer and abort are approximations. The game divides flight into scenes of operation. It does not integrate Earth ascent, heliocentric transfer, atmospheric entry and terminal descent as one trajectory.
- **Descent** — After an authored deorbit or atmospheric braking segment, terminal flight integrates throttle, local gravity, lateral thrust, damping, drag at Mars, changing mass and propellant. A tilted or fast touchdown can damage the mission based on modeled descent and measured terrain slope. Guided is a proportional controller. Expert provides manual controls. There is one site per world and no global surface streaming.
- **Life support** — Cabin pressure faults, battery depletion, suit oxygen, suit charge and crew health affect further operations. Repairs require proximity, diagnostics and a physical spare. The first-person cabin uses a simplified pressurized deck plan. Motion is constrained to walkable geometry. It does not model microgravity locomotion or detailed human physiology.
- **Science and communications** — Terrain products, field data volume and mission completion depend on payload and link design. Low/high orbit and current location affect coverage. Radio transmission time derives from data volume and link rate. The interface displays an illustrative round-trip signal delay, with time compressed in play. Communications use scenario Earth directions and an abstract ground network.

Orbital Frontier is an independent educational game. NASA, USGS, Arizona State University and the mission teams are credited for sourced data. NASA has not endorsed the game.
