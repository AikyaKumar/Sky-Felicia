## Terrain in gameplay

The mission clips **24 × 24** latitude/longitude cells around each target. The nominal centers used before snapping to the source raster are 2.00° N, 19.20° E for the Ritter region, 0.67° N, 23.47° E for the Tranquillitatis plain, and 4.66° S, 28.50° E for the Torricelli region. The in-game labels show the resulting anchor pixel centers. Each tile is about **45 km across**.

The coarse scan block-averages 3 × 3 source cells to an 8 × 8 grid with roughly 5.7 km spacing. Precision preserves the 24 × 24 source cells at about 1.9 km. The scan derives local slopes by finite differences in physical north-south and longitude-adjusted east-west distances. The 90th-percentile slope measures rough terrain in each tile without relying on a single extreme cell. Tile edges are left out of the summary percentile. The **Terrain** view uses the actual retrieved elevations with **8× vertical exaggeration**. The slope values used for scoring use the original scale.

Lidar availability gates the data using altitude, slant range and incidence geometry. The console exposes a coasting estimate for the current orbit. The simulation models acquisition time, energy consumption and data volume. It uses archived elevations as the measurement truth. Available scan resolutions compare broad terrain regions. Features the size of individual landing hazards are below the grid spacing.

## Flight and systems assumptions

- Orbital state uses metres, seconds and double-precision Cartesian vectors. Gravity uses a spherical, point-mass Moon. Velocity Verlet advances the state with substeps of at most 4 seconds while coasting and 0.4 seconds during planned or manual burns. Near an automated ignition it splits the step at the queued time.
- The nav overlay is an impulsive approximation at the configured time-to-ignition. The flight engine actually burns at finite thrust with changing mass, rocket-equation propellant use and a slew-limited in-plane attitude. Direct solar generation and the single-node engine thermal response use game parameters. Module mass, build cost, rates, power draw and conversion overhead are fictional but use real units.
- The planet mesh normalizes lunar radius to one render unit. The spacecraft mockup and map indicators are enlarged for visibility. The lunar texture spins at the sidereal rotation rate. The Sun and Earth directions are fixed for this short scenario. Lunar gravity anomalies, solar and terrestrial perturbations, light-time changes with ephemerides, atmospheric drag, propellant slosh, structure flexibility and six-degree-of-freedom vehicle dynamics are outside this model.
- The initial 23 × 180 km orbit and exact survey tasks are invented. Eclipse comes from geometric lunar occultation of a Sun direction. A fixed 2.565 s round-trip light delay approximates the Moon–Earth link. The acknowledgment requires line of sight, an active radio and electrical power. Site access uses the next revolution of the current orbit and updates as it changes.
- Turndown and fuel cutoffs have physical consequences. A negative-perilune orbit can end in lunar impact. Impact uses the spherical reference body. This flight phase ends at delivery of the science product. It contains no collision geometry for a lander.
